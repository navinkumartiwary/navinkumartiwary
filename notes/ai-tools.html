AI tools: my learning notes

How to get real work done with Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and the best of the rest. Prompting skills, key features, HR prompts you can copy, and exercises to practise with.

By Navin Kumar TiwaryUpdated 26 Nov 202660 min read

I use AI assistants every day in HR and payroll work: for letters, Excel and VBA, policy drafts, analysis and research. These are the notes I keep to get better at it. Each tool has its own tab. Start with Prompting, because it works in every tool, then pick the tool you want to learn.

AI products change every few weeks. Model names, prices and limits here were checked in November 2026. Always trust what your own account and the official pricing page show.

    1Prompting skills that work in every tool

    Foundation

    In one line: the AI only knows what you tell it. Clear context, a clear task and a clear format give you a better answer than any secret trick.

    The six parts of a strong prompt

    PartWhat to writeExample
    Role / audienceWho the answer is for, or what expertise to use"You are helping a Senior HR Officer in Abu Dhabi. The reader is a site worker with basic English."
    TaskOne clear action verb"Draft a warning letter…", "Compare…", "Find errors in…"
    ContextFacts, background, files, rules"Company policy attached. Employee was late 6 times in October."
    FormatLength, structure, tone, file type"One page, formal, Arabic and English side by side, as a Word file."
    ExamplesA sample of what good looks like"Use the style of this previous letter."
    ConstraintsWhat not to do; how to handle gaps"Don't invent facts. If something is missing, ask me first."

    Habits of skilled users

    1. Give the real material. Attach the file, paste the data, share the email thread. Most weak answers come from missing context.
    2. Say why. "This goes to the CEO, so keep it to 5 bullet points" beats "be short".
    3. Ask it to ask you. "Before you start, ask me up to 5 questions you need answered."
    4. Break big jobs into steps. Outline first, approve, then write each section.
    5. Iterate, don't restart. "Good. Make point 3 stronger and cut the last paragraph."
    6. Ask for options. "Give me three subject lines: formal, friendly, urgent."
    7. Make it check itself. "Now review your answer against the policy and list anything that conflicts."
    8. Save what works. Keep a prompt library (OneNote, a Word file, or saved instructions inside the tool).
    9. Use thinking / reasoning modes for maths, logic, law and planning; use fast modes for simple rewrites.
    10. Verify. Check numbers, law articles, names and links yourself before you send anything.

    Prompt patterns

    • Few-shot: give 2–3 examples of input and the output you want, then the new input.
    • Step by step: "Work through this step by step, then give the final answer in one line."
    • Critic: "Act as a strict MOHRE inspector. What problems do you see in this contract?"
    • Rewrite for audience: "Rewrite this for workers with basic English, short sentences, no jargon."
    • Table extraction: "Extract every employee name, ID, date and amount from these PDFs into a table."
    • Template filling: "Here is my template and an Excel list. Make one letter per row."

    Why AI makes mistakes (hallucinations)

    Language models predict likely text. When they don't know, they can produce confident, wrong answers: fake article numbers, invented statistics, broken links. Reduce the risk: give sources, switch on web search or research, ask for citations, ask "how sure are you?", and check high-stakes facts against the original (MOHRE, the law text, your HR system).

    How AI assistants actually work (the expert basics)

    Knowing what happens behind the chat box explains most good and bad results.

    ConceptWhat it meansWhy it matters to you
    Large language model (LLM)A model trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next piece of text. Claude, GPT and Gemini are LLMs.It writes what is likely, not what is checked. Facts need verifying.
    Training and knowledge cutoffThe model learned from data up to a certain date.For anything recent (new laws, prices), switch on web search or give it the document.
    TokensText is split into pieces of about ¾ of a word. Limits and costs are counted in tokens.Long files and long chats use more of your allowance.
    Context windowHow much text the model can "see" at once: your messages, its replies, files and instructions.In very long chats, early details can be summarised or lost. Start fresh chats and keep key facts in a Project.
    Reasoning / thinkingThe model works through the problem in steps before answering.Use it for maths, law, planning and code; skip it for simple rewrites.
    MultimodalReads and makes images, audio, video and files, not just text.Photograph a form, upload a scanned contract, ask about a chart.
    ToolsThe model can call search, run code, read your apps, create files.Tools turn an answer into real work: a spreadsheet, a report, a draft email.
    AgentA model that plans and carries out many tool steps by itself (Claude in Chrome, ChatGPT Work, Gemini Task, Copilot agents).Great for multi-step jobs, but you must check its work and approve sensitive actions.
    MCP (Model Context Protocol)An open standard for connecting AI to apps and data. Claude connectors, ChatGPT plugins and Gemini custom apps can all use MCP servers.One connection method works across many tools.
    RAG (retrieval)The AI searches your documents first, then answers from what it found.This is how Projects, notebooks and company copilots answer from your files.
    MemoryFacts saved about you between chats.Saves repeating yourself; review it so wrong facts don't stick.
    System prompt / instructionsStanding instructions the model follows (project instructions, custom instructions, skills).The best place for rules you always want followed.
    Temperature / variationAnswers are partly random, so the same prompt can give different results.Retry for options; give examples when you need consistency.
    Prompt injectionHidden instructions in a web page or file that try to take over the AI.Be careful letting agents read untrusted sites or emails and act on them.

    Advanced prompting techniques

    TechniqueHowExample
    Structure with tags or headingsSeparate instructions, context and data clearly<policy>…</policy> <question>…</question> or "## Context / ## Task / ## Output"
    Prompt chainingSplit a job into steps, each prompt using the last answer1) extract data from CVs → 2) score → 3) write the shortlist email
    RubricGive the scoring criteria and ask it to grade against them"Score this answer 1–5 on accuracy, clarity and legal correctness, with reasons"
    Output contractDefine the exact format"Return a table with columns: Name, ID, Issue, Action. No other text."
    Meta-promptingAsk the AI to write or improve your prompt"Improve this prompt so the output is more consistent. Explain what you changed."
    Role-play criticsAsk it to attack its own work from a viewpoint"Review this warning letter as a MOHRE inspector and as the employee's lawyer"
    Ground in sourcesTell it to use only the material given and cite it"Answer only from the attached handbook. Quote the clause. Say 'not covered' if it isn't there."
    Ask for uncertaintyMake it flag what it is unsure about"Mark any statement you are less than sure of with (check)."
    Long documents: question lastPut the long text first and your question at the endPaste the policy, then "Using the policy above, …"

    Common mistakes to avoid

    • Vague requests ("make it better") with no audience, purpose or example.
    • Very long chats on many topics, which confuse the model.
    • Trusting numbers, law articles or quotes without checking.
    • Pasting confidential employee data into personal accounts.
    • Asking the AI to decide about people (hire, fire, discipline) instead of helping you decide.
    • Giving up after one weak answer instead of adding context and iterating.

    Practice exercises

    Exercise: turn "summarise this policy" into a strong prompt.

    Model answer: "Summarise the attached leave policy for new joiners. Audience: site supervisors. Give: 1) the 6 rules they must know, as bullet points; 2) a table of leave types with days and pay; 3) three common mistakes to avoid. Keep it under 250 words. Quote the policy clause number for each rule. Don't add anything that isn't in the policy."

    Exercise: write a self-check prompt for a payroll explanation.

    Model answer: "Review your explanation above. Recalculate every number, show the working, list any assumption you made, and flag anything that depends on company policy rather than law."

    Exercise: write a prompt that makes the AI ask questions first.

    Model answer: "I need a 32-slide onboarding deck for new site staff. Before you draft anything, ask me up to 7 questions about audience, content, branding and length. Wait for my answers."

    Find your way around the screen

    • Left sidebar: New chat, your recent chats, Projects, Artifacts (everything Claude has made for you), Scheduled (recurring tasks) and Customize (skills, connectors, plugins).
    • Message box: the + button (attach files, switch connectors and tools, turn on Research or web search), Output (start from a template such as a doc, slides or design), and the model picker.
    • Settings (click your name): Your Claude (memory and chat search), Capabilities (file creation and other features), Usage (how much you have left), Privacy.
    • Ghost icon (top right of a new chat): an incognito chat that isn't saved or remembered.

    Chat basics that save time

    • Attach anything: PDF, Word, Excel, CSV, PowerPoint, images and screenshots. Drag them into the message box. Claude reads tables, scanned forms and charts.
    • Edit your message (pencil icon) to change the question and get a fresh answer, instead of adding corrections underneath.
    • Retry to get a different version of the same answer.
    • Highlight text in a document Claude made and choose Edit with Claude to change just that part.
    • Start a new chat for a new topic. Long chats use more of your allowance and can drift.
    • Voice: on mobile, tap the microphone to dictate or talk.

    Step by step: Projects, Skills, Connectors and Plugins

    These four features turn Claude from a chat box into a working assistant that knows your material, follows your procedures and reaches your apps. Menu names below are from Claude's help centre in November 2026.

    FeatureThink of it asPlans
    ProjectA folder: your files + standing instructions, shared by every chat inside itAll plans (Free: up to 5)
    SkillA saved procedure: "how we do this job", which Claude loads when the job comes upAll plans (needs file creation turned on)
    ConnectorA cable to another app: Google Drive, Gmail, Calendar, Microsoft 365, Canva and many moreAll plans (Free: one custom connector)
    PluginA ready-made kit for one type of work: several skills, connectors and helpers in one installPaid plans

    1 · Projects

    1. In the left sidebar click Projects (or go to claude.ai/projects), then + New Project.
    2. Give it a name and a short description, e.g. "HR Letters". (These are for you; Claude doesn't read them.)
    3. Click Set project instructions and write your standing rules, then Save instructions. Example: "You help with HR letters for a UAE contractor. British spelling. Dates as 25-Nov-26. Never invent salary figures; leave [blanks]. No sign-off unless asked."
    4. In the knowledge panel on the right, click + and upload your templates, policy manual and a couple of good past examples.
    5. Start every related chat inside the project. Each new chat already knows the files and rules.
    6. When Claude gets something wrong twice, add the fix to the instructions rather than repeating it in chat.

    Good projects to create: HR Letters, Payroll Checks, Labour Law Q&A, CIPD Study, Job Search, Excel & VBA. Chats outside a project can't see the project's files.

    2 · Skills

    1. Go to Settings → Capabilities and turn on Create and edit files (code execution). Skills need it.
    2. Open Customize → Skills in the left sidebar. Anthropic's own skills (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF and others) are listed; switch on the ones you want.
    3. Make your own, the easy way: do a job well once in chat (e.g. screening 10 CVs against a scorecard), then say "Turn what we just did into a skill." Claude drafts it and shows a card; review the steps and click save.
    4. Or upload one: a skill is a folder with a SKILL.md file (name, a clear description of when to use it, then the steps), zipped. In Customize → Skills click + → Create skill → Upload a skill, choose the ZIP and switch it on.
    5. Use it by simply asking for the job ("screen these CVs"), or type / in the message box to pick it.

    What a skill file looks like (a simple example you could upload as cv-screening/SKILL.md):

    ---
name: cv-screening
description: Use when screening CVs for site or office roles against
  the 6-point scorecard and producing a ranked Excel shortlist.
---
1. Ask for the job title and the 6 criteria if not given.
2. Read every attached CV. Score each criterion 1-5 with one line
   of evidence quoted from the CV. Never guess missing information.
3. Flag employment gaps over 6 months and unattested degrees.
4. Build an Excel file: one row per candidate, scores, total,
   flags, recommendation (Shortlist / Hold / Reject).
5. Sort by total, highest first. Summarise the top 5 in chat.

    The description decides when Claude uses a skill, so make it specific: "Use when screening CVs for site roles against the 6-point scorecard" works better than "CV helper".

    3 · Connectors

    1. Open Customize → Connectors.
    2. Browse the directory, pick an app (Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar, Microsoft 365, Notion, Canva…) and click Connect. Sign in to that app and approve only the permissions you are comfortable with.
    3. For a service that isn't listed: + Add → Add custom connector, give it a name and paste the remote MCP address the provider gives you.
    4. In any chat, click + (bottom left) → Connectors and switch individual connectors on or off for that conversation.
    5. Ask in plain words: "Find the latest increment file in my Drive and list employees above AED 10,000", "What's on my calendar on Thursday?"

    Connect only apps and servers you trust, and turn off connectors that can write or send when you don't need them. To remove one, go back to Customize → Connectors and click the three dots beside it.

    4 · Plugins

    1. Open Customize in the left sidebar, then the Plugins tab, then Discover.
    2. Pick a plugin for your kind of work (data analysis, design, PDF, writing, research and many more) and click Add. Connect any app it asks for.
    3. In a chat, type / or click + to see the plugin's skills and commands, and click one to see what it does.
    4. To shape a plugin to your own workflow, open it and click Customize, then tell Claude what to change.
    5. Remove or switch off plugins you don't use, so Claude picks the right tool faster.

    Install plugins only from trusted sources. On Team and Enterprise plans, admins can share approved plugins with everyone.

    5 · Memory and chat search

    1. Go to Settings → Your Claude and turn on Generate memory from chats and Search and reference chats.
    2. Click Manage next to Your memory to read what Claude has saved, grouped by topic. Edit or delete anything wrong.
    3. In any chat you can say "remember that I use British spelling" or "forget my old job title".
    4. Ask "what did we decide last week about the overtime report?" and Claude searches your past chats.
    5. Each Project has its own memory. To keep a project separate, turn off Use account memory on the project page.
    6. For something private, start an incognito chat (ghost icon): it isn't saved or remembered.

    6 · Artifacts: documents, slides, designs, dashboards

    TypeUse it forExport as
    DocsPolicies, reports, letters, notes you keep editingWord, PDF, Markdown, Google Docs
    SlidesPresentations, training decksPowerPoint, PDF, Google Slides
    DesignPosters, one-pagers, mock-ups, landing pagesPDF, PowerPoint, HTML, .zip
    Data (paid plans)Dashboards and data storiesShareable page
    Sheets (beta, paid)Spreadsheets with formulas you and Claude edit togetherShareable page
    Whiteboards (beta, paid)Brainstorms, flowcharts, process mapsShareable page
    OtherSmall web tools (calculators, forms), diagrams, codeShareable page
    1. Ask for it in plain words ("make this into a 10-slide deck"), or click Output in the message box and choose a template.
    2. Edit it yourself (type in the doc, change a slide) or ask Claude for changes.
    3. Everything you make is saved in Artifacts in the sidebar.
    4. New artifacts are private until you share them. Use the share button to give a link to colleagues.
    5. Download in the format you need (Word, PowerPoint, PDF) from the artifact's menu.

    Artifacts need Settings → Capabilities → Create and edit files to be on.

    7 · Research and web search

    • Web search: Claude looks things up when your question needs current information. Ask it not to search when you only want it to use your files.
    • Research (paid plans): click + and turn on Research for big questions. Claude runs many searches over several minutes and writes a report with sources. Example: "What changed in UAE private-sector labour rules in 2026? Official sources first."
    • Always open two or three of the sources to check the key facts.

    8 · Claude in Chrome

    1. In Google Chrome, open the Chrome Web Store, search Claude, click Add to Chrome and sign in (paid plans).
    2. Pin it: puzzle-piece icon → pin next to Claude. Click the icon to open the side panel.
    3. Give it a task on the page you're viewing: "Collect the title, company, emirate and link of every HR job on this page into a table."
    4. Watch the first few tasks. Claude pauses for your approval on sensitive actions; stop it if anything looks wrong.
    5. Save a prompt that works as a shortcut and reuse it by typing /. The clock icon in the panel lets you run shortcuts on a schedule.

    Be careful on sites with sign-ins, payments or personal data. Never let it submit anything you haven't reviewed.

    9 · Claude for Microsoft 365 (Excel, Word, PowerPoint, Outlook)

    1. In Excel (and Word, PowerPoint, Outlook) go to Home → Add-ins (Windows) or Tools → Add-ins (Mac), search Claude and add it. Sign in with your Claude account (paid plans).
    2. Open the Claude panel inside the app and ask: "Add a column that calculates gratuity from basic salary and joining date", "Build a pivot of overtime by site and month", "Explain this formula".
    3. Turn on Let Claude work across files in the add-in settings to move information between apps: "Use the figures in the open workbook to update the chart on slide 4."
    4. Your enabled skills work inside the add-ins too.

    Claude only sees the files and emails that are open. For work files, use the setup your company approves and check with IT first.

    10 · Scheduled tasks

    1. In any chat, describe the job and how often: "Every Monday at 8 AM, list new MOHRE announcements from the past week with links." Answer Claude's questions and click Schedule.
    2. Or click Scheduled in the sidebar → New task → Create with Claude or Set up manually (name, prompt, frequency: hourly, daily, weekly, weekdays).
    3. Tasks run in the cloud even when your computer is off, unless they need files on your computer.
    4. From Scheduled you can see past runs, edit, pause, delete or run a task now.

    11 · Claude Code (for building tools)

    Claude Code is the version of Claude that writes, runs and fixes code in a project folder: Python scripts, Excel automation, websites. Open the Code area of the desktop app or claude.ai/code, choose a folder or GitHub repository, and describe what you want: "Create a script that makes increment letters from this Excel file and the Word template, and test it on the sample rows." It shows each change, runs the code and can save the work to GitHub.

    12 · Usage limits and long chats

    • Paid plans have a session limit that resets every five hours (counted from your first message) and a weekly limit. Check Settings → Usage.
    • Usage is shared across the web, desktop, mobile and Claude Code.
    • Make it last longer: use a lower effort for routine jobs, turn off thinking when not needed, switch off connectors and web search you don't need, keep project instructions short, remove old project files, and start a new chat when one gets long.
    • The context window (how much one chat can hold) is very large on the newest models. When a chat gets near the limit, Claude summarises earlier parts automatically.

    13 · Privacy settings

    • Settings → Privacy: choose whether your chats may be used to improve Claude.
    • Use incognito chats for anything you don't want saved.
    • Delete chats or memory topics you no longer want kept.
    • For company data, use a Team or Enterprise account if your employer provides one.

    How to get the best from Claude

    • One Project per repeated area of work; one skill per repeated procedure; connectors only for the apps you really use.
    • Give templates and examples; Claude follows formatting closely when it sees a sample.
    • For files, say the format: "Give me this as an Excel file with formulas, not values."
    • For long jobs, ask for a plan or outline first, then approve.
    • Turn on Research or web search for anything current (laws, prices, news).
    • Edit memory when something is wrong; tell it "remember…" or "forget…" directly.
    • Use the thumbs-down button to report poor answers.

    HR prompts to copy

    • "Using the attached policy manual, draft a reply to this employee's question about sick leave. Quote the clause. Simple English, under 120 words."
    • "Here is last month's payroll and this month's. List every employee whose net pay changed by more than 10% and the likely reason."
    • "Write a Python script that reads my salary increment list in Excel, fills the Word template for every row where STATUS is blank, saves each letter as 'Name - Position.docx' and marks STATUS 'Made'."
    • "Turn these interview notes into a scored candidate summary against the job's 5 competencies, with evidence for each score."

    Practice exercises

    Exercise: set up an HR Letters Project in Claude.

    Steps: create a Project; upload your letter templates and policy manual; write project instructions (tone, spelling, date format, "never invent salary figures", file naming rule); start a chat and ask for one letter; correct anything wrong and add the correction to the instructions. Done when: a new chat produces a correct letter without you repeating the rules.

    Exercise: build a dashboard artifact from sample data.

    Upload a sample headcount sheet (IDs starting EMP) and ask: "Build an interactive dashboard with headcount by site, nationality and department, monthly turnover, and filters. Use only this data." Check every number against the sheet using a pivot table.

    Exercise: connect Google Drive and use it in a chat.

    Customize → Connectors → Google Drive → Connect. In a new chat: "Find the most recent file in my Drive with 'headcount' in the name and summarise it in 5 bullet points." Then switch the connector off in the + menu and notice the difference.

    Exercise: Claude in Excel.

    Open a sample payroll workbook (EMP IDs). Ask the Claude panel: "Add a gratuity column using basic salary and joining date, flag anyone with more than 5 years' service, and explain the formula." Check three rows by hand.

    Exercise: set up a weekly scheduled task.

    "Every Sunday at 7 PM, give me 10 bullet points of UAE HR and labour news from the past week with links." Read the first three runs carefully before relying on it, and adjust the prompt if it picks weak sources.

    Exercise: review and clean your memory.

    Settings → Your Claude → Manage memory. Read every topic, delete anything outdated, and correct anything wrong. Then ask Claude in a chat: "What do you know about how I like my letters formatted?"

    Exercise: turn a repeated task into a skill.

    After you have done a task well three times (e.g. CV screening), ask: "Turn what we just did into a skill with clear steps, inputs and checks." Review the steps carefully, save it, then test it on a new batch.

    3ChatGPT (OpenAI): the complete guide

    All-rounder · voice · images · agents

    Best at: fast everyday help, natural voice conversation, image creation and editing, quick data analysis with charts, and a huge library of plugins. It is the most widely used assistant, so many colleagues will already know it. ChatGPT changed a lot in 2026 (new GPT-6 models, "Work" replacing agent mode, apps renamed plugins), so older tutorials on YouTube are often out of date.

    Step by step: Projects, personalisation, Work, plugins and tasks

    1 · Projects

    1. In the sidebar click New project, name it (e.g. "CIPD Assignment") and pick a colour or icon.
    2. Open ••• → Project settings and add instructions (how ChatGPT should answer inside this project).
    3. Add files: the brief, your notes, templates. Limits: 5 files on Free, 25 on Go and Plus, 40 on Pro and business plans.
    4. Under Project settings → Memory, choose project-only memory if you want this project kept separate.
    5. Start chats inside the project, or drag an existing chat into it. You can invite collaborators to share a project.

    2 · Custom instructions, personality and memory

    1. Go to Settings → Personalization.
    2. Fill in Custom instructions (up to 5,000 characters on paid plans): who you are, what you do, how you want answers.
    3. Pick a base style (Default, Professional, Friendly, Candid, Efficient and others) and adjust warmth, enthusiasm and emojis.
    4. Under Memory, check Reference saved memories and Reference chat history, then click Manage to review and delete entries.
    5. Use a Temporary chat for anything you don't want remembered.

    3 · Work (ChatGPT's agent)

    1. Switch the message box from Chat to Work.
    2. Describe the finished result, not the clicks: "Using the two attached files, build an Excel report of overtime hours by site and month, with a summary tab and three insights."
    3. Use plan mode to see and approve its plan first.
    4. It asks before visiting new websites and always before payments or submitting forms. You can take over if it gets stuck.
    5. Check the output against your source files. Work and Codex share one usage allowance.

    4 · Plugins (ChatGPT's connectors)

    1. Click Plugins in the sidebar (or Settings → Plugins) and browse the directory.
    2. Open a plugin, review what it includes (apps and skills) and click Install. If it needs an account, click Connect and sign in.
    3. In a chat, type @ and the plugin name, or just ask in plain words.
    4. Paid plans can add a custom MCP server from the plugin settings (developer mode).
    5. If you built custom GPTs, open each one and use Migrate to plugin before 11 December 2026.

    5 · Deep research

    1. Type @Deepresearch (or choose it from +) and write a clear research question with scope: country, time period, preferred sources.
    2. Review the research plan it proposes and adjust it.
    3. Wait 5–30 minutes; you can leave and come back.
    4. Open the cited sources for the key claims before you use the report.

    6 · Scheduled tasks

    1. Ask in a chat: "Every Monday at 8 AM, send me 10 bullet points of UAE HR news from the past week with links."
    2. Confirm the schedule. Manage tasks from Scheduled in the sidebar or Settings → Notifications → Manage tasks.
    3. Free runs at most daily; paid plans allow hourly and exact times. Plus allows 5 active tasks.

    7 · Images, voice and data analysis

    • Images: describe the picture, size and text exactly ("A4 poster, heading 'Stay Hydrated', simple icons, English and Arabic"). Upload a photo to edit it. Always check spelling in images, especially Arabic.
    • Voice: tap the voice button. Interrupt any time. On paid plans, turn on the camera or share your screen and ask about what you see.
    • Data analysis: upload Excel or CSV and say "Show the Python you used". Ask for the cleaned file back so you can check it.

    Privacy and data

    • Settings → Data controls → Improve the model for everyone: turn off if you don't want your chats used for training (personal plans).
    • Temporary chats are not used for training; a copy may be kept for up to 30 days for safety.
    • Business, Enterprise and Edu workspaces are not trained on by default.
    • Manage or delete shared links in Data controls.

    Usage limits

    Normal text chat is effectively unlimited on all plans, but there are caps on uploads, images, voice minutes, data analysis, deep research, higher thinking levels, Pro models, and the shared Work/Codex allowance. Deep research allowances reset 30 days after first use. When you hit a cap, ChatGPT tells you when it resets.

    • What it is: an answer engine. Every answer comes with numbered sources you can open.
    • How to use: ask a question at perplexity.ai; use follow-up questions to dig deeper; switch to the deeper Research mode for reports; create Spaces to keep a topic's threads, files and instructions together.
    • Best HR uses: "What changed in UAE labour rules this month?", salary benchmark articles, quick fact checks with sources.
    • Watch out: it summarises what it finds; low-quality sites can still appear, so prefer official sources.

    Meta AI (inside WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook)

    • What it is: Meta's assistant, available from the blue circle icon or by typing @Meta AI in chats.
    • How to use: quick questions, translations and images without leaving WhatsApp.
    • Watch out: don't share employee information in it; for work, use your approved tools.

    Grok (xAI)

    • What it is: an assistant built into X (Twitter) and grok.com, with live access to X posts and an image/video generator.
    • Best for: what people are saying right now about a topic.
    • Watch out: social posts are not reliable sources; verify facts elsewhere.

    DeepSeek, Qwen and other open models

    • What they are: strong, low-cost models, some released as "open weights" that companies can run on their own servers.
    • Best for: developers and companies that want private, self-hosted AI.
    • Watch out: check where the consumer apps store data before entering anything personal or confidential.

    Mistral Le Chat

    • What it is: a fast European assistant with web search, document reading and images.
    • Best for: a quick second opinion on writing; organisations that prefer European providers.

    Creative and video tools

    • Canva Magic Studio: type a description to get posters, social posts and certificates in your brand colours; Magic Write drafts text; background remover and resize for every platform.
    • Gamma: paste notes or an outline and get a full deck, document or web page; edit cards, change theme, export to PowerPoint or PDF.
    • HeyGen and Synthesia: write a script, choose an avatar and language, and get a presenter video. Good for training in several languages. Use your own likeness only with consent.
    • ElevenLabs: realistic voice-overs and dubbing into other languages; you can clone your own voice for training videos.
    • Midjourney: high-quality artistic images from detailed prompts; less suited to text-heavy designs.

    Automation tools

    • Power Automate: Microsoft 365 flows, e.g. new Microsoft Form response → save PDF to SharePoint → email HR. Has AI steps for extracting data from documents.
    • Zapier, Make and n8n: connect apps with triggers and actions, and add an AI step in the middle (summarise, classify, draft). n8n can be self-hosted for privacy.
    • Google Apps Script: free automation inside Sheets, Forms and Gmail; Gemini or Claude can write the script for you.

    Before using any tool with work data, check its privacy settings, where data is stored and whether your company allows it.

    8Using AI safely and responsibly at work

    Privacy · accuracy · ethics

    In one line: you are responsible for what you send, not the AI. Protect people's data, check facts, and keep a human in charge of decisions about people.

    Data rules

    • Follow your company's AI policy. If there isn't one, help write it.
    • Don't paste passport numbers, Emirates IDs, bank accounts, medical details or salary lists of named people into personal, free AI accounts. Use company-approved tools, or remove names (use EMP IDs or sample data).
    • Check settings: turn off "use my data for training" on personal plans; business plans usually exclude training by default.
    • The UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) and free-zone laws apply to personal data you process.

    Accuracy rules

    • Check every legal article, number, date and name against the source.
    • Ask for sources and open them.
    • Re-run calculations in Excel for anything involving money.
    • Be careful with very recent changes; switch on search or research.

    Fairness and ethics

    • AI can repeat bias found in its training data (e.g. in CV screening). Use clear, job-related criteria, review outputs for patterns, and make the final decision yourself.
    • Tell people when AI has been used in decisions that affect them, where appropriate.
    • Don't use AI to create fake documents, reviews or records.
    • Respect copyright: don't ask for whole books or paid content.

    Practice exercise

    Exercise: anonymise a dataset before analysis.

    Take a real report, replace names with EMP IDs, remove passport, Emirates ID and bank columns, round salaries into bands if not needed, and keep the key file (ID to name) only on your company drive. Then run the analysis in the AI tool and map results back yourself.

    Exercise: draft a one-page AI use policy for HR.

    Ask Claude: "Draft a one-page AI acceptable-use policy for an HR department in the UAE: approved tools, data that must never be entered, checking rules, human decision-making, disclosure, and who to ask." Mark it as a draft for management review.

    9Practice projects and skill check

    Learn by doing

    How to use: complete one project a week. Tick each one when it works on real (or sample) data and you have checked the output.

    Project 1: a personal HR assistant as a Claude Project and a Gemini skill.

    Set up the same assistant in both tools with your rules and policy files. Ask both 10 real questions. Record which was more accurate and why. Keep the better one as your daily assistant.

    Project 2: letter automation end to end.

    Excel list → Python or Claude → Word letters → STATUS marked "Made" → PDF copies. Write a one-page "how to run it" guide so a colleague can use it. Test with 5 sample rows including edge cases (blank dates, missing allowances).

    Project 3: research report with sources.

    Use Claude Research, ChatGPT deep research and Perplexity on the same question, e.g. "Changes to UAE private-sector labour rules in 2026". Merge into one report, verify every claim against MOHRE or official sources, and note where the tools disagreed.

    Project 4: a training video.

    Script with Claude, slides with Claude Slides or Gamma, voice with ElevenLabs or your own voice, clips with Google Flow, edit in CapCut or Clipchamp. Keep it under 5 minutes and add a 3-question quiz.

    Project 5: an HR dashboard.

    Use sample data (EMP IDs). Build it as a Claude dashboard artifact, then rebuild the same view in Power BI. Compare effort, flexibility and how easy each is to share.

    Project 6: a scheduled task.

    Create a weekly scheduled task: "Every Monday at 8 AM, list new MOHRE announcements and UAE HR news from the past week with links, in 10 bullet points." Review the first three runs for accuracy before relying on it.

    Skill check: can you…

    • Write a prompt with role, task, context, format, examples and constraints without thinking about it?
    • Explain when to use Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and NotebookLM?
    • Set up a Project, a skill, a connector and custom instructions?
    • Get a cited research report and check its sources?
    • Produce a finished Word, Excel or PowerPoint file from a prompt?
    • Automate one repeated HR task and explain how you check it?
    • Explain the data rules you follow?
